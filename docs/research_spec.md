## Problem and outputs

**Paper.** Given recordings `y_n,m(t)` for N sound events and M microphones, recover 3D positions of point-like sources `b_n`, point-like microphones `s_m`, and K planar boundaries `r_k`. Sources and microphones are omnidirectional. The room is a convex polyhedron with known number of faces K. Source positions, microphone positions, source emission times, and per-microphone timing offsets are unknown. The emitted waveform is known. The solution is only determined up to a global rigid transform absent external geometric information.

The plane is encoded by a 3-vector `r_k` normal to the plane, whose norm is the plane distance from the coordinate origin. The paper places the coordinate center at the room center for its plane error reporting, but does not make this a known input to the uncalibrated problem.

## Signal and image-source model

Direct-path time of flight (TOF), Eq. (1):

`tau_(n,m,0) = || b_n - s_m ||_2 / c`,

where c is sound speed. For a plane parameterized by `r`, the image operator, Eq. (2), is:

`IM(b,r) = b + 2 (1 - r^T b / ||r||^2) r`.

This is reflection across the plane `r^T x = ||r||^2` under the paper's parameterization. First-order image source: `p_(n,k) = IM(b_n,r_k)`. Second-order image source for ordered distinct planes, Eq. (3): `p_(n,k1,k2) = IM(p_(n,k1), r_(k2))`; there are `K(K-1)` such ordered pairs. Direct, first-order, and second-order TOFs are Euclidean distance from the real microphone to the corresponding real/virtual source divided by c (Eq. 4).

Measured arrival time is TOF plus emission time and microphone clock offset (Eqs. 5–7): `tau_a = tau + tau_e,n + tau_o,m`. The modeled signal (Eq. 9) is a sum of delayed, scaled impulses for direct and first-/second-order paths, plus a residual term for higher-order reflections, boundary effects, objects, etc., convolved with the emitted signal after common source and microphone responses are folded into it (Eq. 8). Amplitudes are frequency independent. The model neglects frequency-dependent propagation attenuation, nonlinear effects, and frequency-dependent reflections. Only the early response is approximated with the image model, under convex planar geometry and wavelengths small relative to reflector surface areas.

## Chirp, matched filtering, and TOA

**Paper.** The emitted frequency sweep is printed as `x(t) = sin(2 pi f0 t + f1 t^2)`, with interval `t in [0, (f1-f0)/(2 f1)]` (Eq. 10). The text calls `f0` and `f1` the initial and final instantaneous frequencies. The 2017 ScienceDirect indexed text repeats this same printed expression and description; it does not clarify the inconsistency ([journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016003217305458)).

**Engineering interpretation / dimensional audit.** A sine argument must be dimensionless. With `f0` and `f1` both interpreted as frequencies (Hz), `2 pi f0 t` is dimensionless but `f1 t^2` has units of seconds, and the stated interval has units of seconds only algebraically if numerator and denominator are both frequency (but its relation to a sweep duration remains unexplained). No corrected chirp law is inferred here. Preserve the printed expression pending source clarification.

The matched filter is the time-reversed transmitted signal with its spectrum normalized by spectrum magnitude, following generalized cross-correlation (Eq. 11). Convolution produces compressed pulses whose peaks mark TOAs. Peak finder Algorithm 1: in a sliding window of length T, retain samples at/above `thr1` times that window's local maximum (non-maximum suppression); then retain only samples at/above `thr2` times the global maximum; output remaining nonzero sample times. T is roughly compressed-pulse length. `thr1` is near 1; `thr2` is selected in relation to SNR to trade missed weak echoes against noise peaks. Each source/microphone pair can have a different detected peak count, allowing missing and spurious detections.

## Initial source/microphone localization

The first detected peak is assumed to be direct path because it is shortest and likely strongest. Convert its arrival time to range using `d_(n,m)=c tau_a,n,m,1` (Eq. 12), then form the differenced squared-distance matrix `D_(n,m)=d_(n,m)^2-d_(1,m)^2-d_(n,1)^2` (Eq. 13; reference indices are first source and first microphone).

With zero emission/offset times and noiseless ranges, D has rank 3 and factors as `B^T S` (Eqs. 13–14). Truncated SVD gives a factorization, but the factors have an arbitrary invertible 3x3 transform Q. Solve for Q using quadratic distance constraints (referenced method [28]) to recover Euclidean coordinates. For unknown emission and offset times, the paper adapts Gaubitch et al. [7]: iteratively form corrected `D_hat = D + phi(tau_e,tau_o)`, project to closest rank-3 matrix, use residual in a linear least-squares update of event emission times and microphone offsets, update phi, and repeat until Frobenius residual falls below a threshold (Algorithm 2). Then solve the Q constraints. This is an initialization, not the final estimate. TOF estimates are obtained by subtracting estimated emission and offset from each arrival time (Eq. 15).

**Open.** The preprint does not print the explicit `phi` entries, the linear system, its initialization, convergence threshold, or the full Q recovery equations; these are delegated to cited works. These are required for faithful implementation and must be recovered from the cited sources or authors' code, not guessed.

## Greedy plane search and echo assignment

The plane search avoids combinatorial echo labeling. Discretize candidate plane vectors on a 3D Cartesian grid bounded by a coarse room-size guess. Grid spacing follows desired precision and compute budget. Initialize candidate image-source set with real sources. At iteration j, evaluate each grid candidate r against all microphones, sources, and previously available real/virtual sources using geometry TOFs `||s_m - IM(p_n,k,r)||/c` (Eq. 16). For each geometry TOF choose nearest measured TOA (Eq. 17). Score each match with `exp(-delta_tau^2/(2 sigma^2)) + epsilon` (Eq. 18); aggregate over n,m and existing image-source indices by product (Eq. 19). Choose the candidate according to Eq. 20 and record labels. The printed Eq. 20 appears to say `arg min` even though the text says maximum score; this remains unresolved.

After a plane is accepted, remove measured TOAs within `thr3` of any currently predicted geometry TOF (Eqs. 22–23), and add its virtual sources so later iterations can exploit second-order paths. Thus second-order reflections are excluded from the first plane search and introduced incrementally. **Engineering interpretation:** `delta_tau` and `sigma` have units of time, so the exponent is dimensionless; `epsilon` is added to an exponential score and is therefore a dimensionless score floor, not a time. The paper uses it to lower-bound the score. This greedy approach is not a guarantee of globally optimal plane ordering or geometry.

## Global nonlinear refinement

Using initial positions and plane estimates, generate direct, all first-order, and ordered distinct second-order geometry TOFs (Eqs. 24–26). Match each to its nearest signal TOA and set a binary indicator to 1 only when timing difference is under `thr3` (Eqs. 27–28); this prunes unmatched predictions and handles missing peaks. Reconstruct corresponding arrival times by adding estimated emission and clock offsets (Eq. 29). Then jointly minimize squared timing residuals over source positions, microphone positions, plane vectors, emission times, and offsets (Eq. 30), using a standard nonlinear least-squares solver, e.g. MATLAB `lsqnonlin` with Levenberg–Marquardt. Initialization is the preceding staged result. The paper reports the delay-to-plane/source image geometry as already labeled before this continuous refinement.

**Open.** The text does not report exact solver tolerances, scaling, bounds, stopping rules, or how assignments are refreshed (if at all) during optimization. This objective is non-convex. Robustness claims refer to the preceding peak pruning/robust score stages; Eq. 30 itself is ordinary indicator-weighted least squares.

## Reported settings and experiments

### Synthetic

**Paper.** 30 randomly generated six-face convex rooms. Start from a 6 m cube; perturb plane-normal length uniformly in [-1.5 m, 1.5 m] and orientation uniformly in [-20°, 20°]. Microphone and source positions sampled uniformly inside room volume. Tests use 12 or 8 microphones and 20 sources. A 5 s chirp sweeps 3–6 kHz. Image-method room impulse responses; added white noise at SNR 14, 6, 0, -6, -14 dB. Peak thresholds `thr1=0.9`, `thr2=0.1`; grid spacing 0.2 m; `epsilon=0.1` (dimensionless score floor); `sigma=1.47e-4 s` (reported as 0.05 m); `thr3=5.88e-4 s` (reported as 0.2 m). The paper's printed `0.1s` for epsilon is dimensionally inconsistent with Eq. 18; the equation and its additive score role support a dimensionless value, and no other value is substituted.

MATLAB runtime on 2.6 GHz CPU: about 258 s for 20 sources/12 microphones: matched filtering 6 s, peak finding 2 s, initial positions 40 s, grid plane search 150 s, final refinement 60 s. Errors for positions use Procrustes alignment. Plane orientation is normal-vector angle; plane offset error is difference in normal-vector length. Inlier cutoffs: position/plane distance below 0.5 m and plane angle below 20°. Reported mean inlier errors at reasonable SNR: with 12 mics, microphone 0.015 m, source 0.015 m, plane angle 0.5°, plane offset 0.01 m; with 8 mics, 0.035 m, 0.05 m, 2°, 0.02 m. Outliers below 10% (12 mics), below 20% (8 mics). Errors rise abruptly at -14 dB; 12 microphones generally outperform 8. These are the paper's results and are not BatSight validation.

### Real room

**Paper.** A historic room in Genoa, approx. 8.5 x 7.5 x 7 m, with vaulted ceiling and wooden floor. 12 radio-frequency omnidirectional lavalier microphones in a central approx. 3 x 3 m region, heights 0.2–2 m; a 3 cm wireless speaker at 17 locations; same chirp. Synchronous National Instruments PXI acquisition. Traffic noise was present. Matched filter acquired from another transmission in an anechoic setting with a microphone close to source, to include common speaker/microphone responses. Ground truth: Leica C10 laser scan, manually divided into six surfaces and plane-fit; VICON with 8 BONITA cameras and markers for mic/source locations, registered to scan using six shared floor markers.

Table 1 column values: sources mean 59 mm/std 31 mm; microphones mean 31 mm/std 22 mm. Nearby prose says “average error of 22 mm” for microphones and “31 mm” for sources. Those prose figures match Table 1's standard deviations, not its means; this is a statistic/label inconsistency, not just a swapped source/microphone label. Keep the table transcription and prose statement separate until the final journal page or authors clarify. Refined plane errors (Table 2), angle / offset: walls 0.89°/22 mm, 1.72°/34 mm, 0.63°/62 mm, 0.43°/4 mm; floor 1.40°/1 mm; ceiling 2.98°/1824 mm. The large ceiling discrepancy is attributed to vaulted, non-planar ceiling violating the piecewise-planar model and making height ambiguous. Initial grid-search plane errors are less precise (Tables 3).
