# Assumptions and limitations

## Paper-backed assumptions

- The room is a convex polyhedron and its face count K is known.
- Source and microphone locations are unknown; they are modeled as point-like and omnidirectional.
- The transmitted signal is known. Emission times and per-microphone clock offsets are modeled and estimated.
- Propagation speed is treated as a known coefficient c for distance conversion.
- Early reflections are approximated by specular image sources for planar surfaces; surfaces act as perfect acoustic mirrors in that construction.
- Frequency-dependent propagation/reflection effects and nonlinearities are neglected. Higher-order effects and objects are residual terms; geometry is explicitly modeled through second order.
- The first detected peak is taken as direct path. Missing and spurious peak detections are expected and handled heuristically/robustly in initialization and assignment.
- The nonlinear objective can have ambiguous solutions; initialization quality affects final refinement. There is a global rigid-coordinate ambiguity without external reference.
- The measured real room includes non-planarity (vaulted ceiling/niches), a direct model mismatch that substantially affected the ceiling estimate.

## Product boundaries for BatSight (engineering proposal)

- **Laptop recording mode:** one built-in microphone is suitable for recording a chirp, inspecting waveform/spectrogram, and exploring echo/TOA detections. Report it as a single-channel acoustic experiment. It must not be labeled as the paper's full uncalibrated 3D reconstruction or imply that the multichannel self-localization and plane inference are determined from that recording.
- **Research multichannel mode:** keep separate and require a documented set of synchronized or timing-offset-modeled microphone channels, multiple source events, known emitted waveform, known face count, and convex-room assumption before offering a paper-method reconstruction experiment.
- Preserve raw recordings and metadata, record the exact paper version and all parameter values, and distinguish a paper-reported number from a BatSight measured result.
- Do not add learned/neural reconstruction as a substitute for the paper's method.

## Phase 6D calibrated recording-session boundaries

- The recording-session workflow consumes explicit microphone and source/event positions, event emission times, a named shared time reference, sample rate, sound speed, reference waveform description, per-recording start time, and channel-to-microphone mapping. These are operator-provided calibration facts; BatSight does not infer missing values.
- Multichannel extraction requires metadata to assert synchronized clocks and include an evidence/acquisition description. The software records that this assertion came from metadata and does not independently verify synchronization. Files in one session are not assumed synchronized without those explicit conditions.
- WAV sample rates must match the declared session rate exactly. No implicit resampling, channel relabeling, timing alignment, or position estimation occurs.
- Single laptop microphone recording remains a separate `laptop_single_device` provenance and cannot pass calibrated multichannel array extraction validation.
- Phase 6D reuses Phase 5E.1 and returns direct-path candidates or missing states, unclassified echo candidates, rejected-peak records, and signal diagnostics. It does not claim a real echo is first order, assign wall identity, or estimate geometry. Synthetic integration tests validate software behavior only, not real-world measurement accuracy.
- Generic reference sine/sweep settings are engineering inputs and do not resolve or reproduce the 2016 paper's ambiguous chirp equation.

## What is not guaranteed

The method does not establish support for arbitrary non-convex spaces, unknown chirps/natural signals, unknown face count, arbitrary frequency-dependent materials, diffuse scattering, moving microphones during an acquisition without a motion model, or a single microphone full 3D solution. The paper does not guarantee a unique or globally optimal answer, reliable detection in every SNR, or accuracy outside its evaluation settings. The synthetic reported accuracy is not a general accuracy claim. The real experiment's ceiling error demonstrates sensitivity to structural mismatch.

## Research verification and implementation gates

The 2016 arXiv v1 PDF is archived at `research/Uncalibrated_3D_Room_Reconstruction_from_Sound.pdf`. The distinct 2017 JFI article is identifiable as DOI [10.1016/j.jfranklin.2017.10.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfranklin.2017.10.024), Journal of the Franklin Institute 354(18), 8678–8709. The final publisher PDF is access-controlled and the University of Genoa repository lists its copy as closed access. Public publisher metadata/abstract and indexed snippets establish the later publication and an added claim of comparison with two methods, but do not support a complete equation-by-equation or results comparison. Do not declare either version superseded; preserve their provenance separately.

**Phase 2 decision:** source verification now supports the rank-3 projection and quadratic factor-recovery ingredients, and supports using textual `arg max` as an explicitly documented correction. It does not support exact 2016 initialization reproduction, an unambiguous chirp generator, exact 2017-versus-2016 change claims, or dataset reuse without access/license confirmation. These are blockers for a faithful end-to-end reconstruction milestone, though a separately scoped, non-reconstruction signal-analysis milestone may proceed if it uses a clearly specified waveform and does not claim fidelity to Eq. 10.
